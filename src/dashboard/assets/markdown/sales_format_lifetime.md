## The rise and fall of music formats

Every format in this chart tells a story about how people wanted to listen to music.

Vinyl dominated for decades, valued for its sound quality and artwork. The Cassette arrived in the 1960s as a portable alternative, peaking in the late 1980s before being overtaken by the CD. The CD, developed by Philips and Sony and released in 1982, reached its peak in 1999 and 2000, a revenue milestone no other format has matched since.

The rise of the internet changed everything. Downloads disrupted physical sales in the early 2000s, and streaming has since become the dominant force in the industry. By 2021, streaming accounted for 76% of total music industry revenue.

Meanwhile, Vinyl never truly disappeared. It remains the only format in this dataset with an unbroken presence from 1973 to 2019 and continues to grow today.

### What is 8-Track?

If you were not around in the 1970s, you have probably never encountered an 8-Track. It was a magnetic tape cartridge format popular in the US from the mid-1960s to the late 1970s, designed primarily for cars after Ford offered players as a standard option in 1966.

Its heyday lasted from 1968 to 1975, and by 1980 it was effectively obsolete.  The format had several critical flaws. You could not rewind. Songs were sometimes split mid-track across program changes with an audible click and a fade. If the playback heads became even slightly misaligned, two songs would bleed into each other at equal volume.  The internal components degraded quickly, and tapes had a reputation for jamming and becoming tangled. The Cassette, which was smaller, cheaper and allowed rewinding, made the 8-Track obsolete almost overnight.
